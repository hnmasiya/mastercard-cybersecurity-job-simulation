# Mastercard Cybersecurity Job Simulation — Forage

**Completed:** September 20, 2026  
**Platform:** Forage  
**Program:** Mastercard Cybersecurity Job Simulation

## Scenario

A simulated Security Awareness Team exercise focused on phishing risk, phishing-simulation design, campaign-result analysis, and security-awareness training.

## Business Context

The exercise addressed the human-risk side of cybersecurity: helping employees recognize social-engineering techniques and using simulated phishing results to identify where additional awareness training should be prioritized.

> **Evidence boundary:** This was a controlled Forage virtual job simulation. It was not employment with Mastercard and does not represent a real Mastercard security incident or production engagement.

## Investigation / Task Analysis

### Task 1 — Design a Phishing Email Simulation

Created a believable simulated phishing email based on an account-security scenario.

The exercise required:
- Adding relevant employee context.
- Using professional spelling and grammar.
- Adding legitimacy cues.
- Masking the hyperlink behind descriptive text.
- Applying common phishing tactics such as urgency, trusted-team impersonation and account-security themes.

The exercise also explicitly noted that a real phishing simulation should use an authorized training destination rather than collect real credentials.

[View Task 1](task-1/phishing-simulation.md)

### Task 2 — Interpret Phishing Simulation Results

Analyzed the supplied campaign results to determine which teams showed the highest phishing success rates and used the results to develop targeted awareness recommendations.

[View Task 2](task-2/phishing-awareness-training.md)

## Evidence / Data

The supplied campaign results showed:

| Team | Email Open Rate | Click-Through Rate | Phishing Success Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| IT | 80% | 2% | 0% |
| HR | 100% | 85% | 75% |
| Card Services | 60% | 50% | 10% |
| Reception | 40% | 10% | 0% |
| Engineering | 70% | 4% | 1% |
| Marketing | 65% | 40% | 38% |
| R&D | 50% | 5% | 2% |
| Overall average | 66% | 28% | 18% |

## Findings

- **HR** recorded the highest phishing success rate at **75%**.
- **Marketing** recorded the second-highest phishing success rate at **38%**.
- Both were above the supplied overall phishing success rate of **18%**.
- HR also had the highest click-through rate at **85%**.
- Marketing recorded a **40%** click-through rate.

## Indicators of Suspicion / Risk

The simulated phishing content demonstrated:
- Urgency and time pressure.
- Impersonation of a trusted internal support function.
- Account-security/password-reset themes.
- Masked hyperlinks.
- A request to take action through an account-recovery workflow.

These were learning examples rather than evidence of a real attack.

## Analysis

The campaign results were used to distinguish higher-risk teams from the overall simulated population and connect quantitative results with targeted awareness interventions.

## Recommended Actions

### HR
Prioritize training on urgent account-security requests, impersonation and social engineering, sender/link verification, suspicious-email reporting, and safe handling of sensitive information.

### Marketing
Prioritize training on suspicious links and unexpected requests, urgency and social engineering, trusted-channel verification, phishing reporting, and reinforcement through recurring simulations.

### Program-level measurement
Repeat controlled simulations and compare click-through rate, phishing success rate, reporting rate, and results by team.

## Lessons Learned

- Phishing risk can vary significantly between teams.
- Campaign data can be converted into targeted security-awareness priorities.
- Realistic phishing simulations should remain controlled and use authorized training destinations.
- Awareness programs should measure improvement over repeated simulations.
