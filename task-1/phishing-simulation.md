# Task 1 — Design a Phishing Email Simulation

## Objective
Create a believable simulated phishing email for a security-awareness campaign.

## Approach
The original example was an obvious fake. The exercise required improving it by:
- Adding relevant context for a Mastercard employee.
- Masking the hyperlink within descriptive text.
- Using correct spelling and grammar.
- Adding points of legitimacy.

## Improved simulation
**From:** Mastercard IT Support support@mastercard.com  
**To:** Employee <employee@example.com>  
**Subject:** Password Reset Required — Immediate Attention Needed

> Dear Employee,
>
> As part of Mastercard’s ongoing security monitoring, we recently identified unusual sign-in activity associated with your employee account. As a precaution, we are requiring affected employees to verify their account and reset their password.
>
> Please complete the password reset process using the secure account recovery portal below:
>
> **[Review Account Security and Reset Password]**
>
> For your protection, please complete this process within the next hour. If the required action is not completed, access to some Mastercard services may be temporarily restricted while your account is reviewed.
>
> If you have already completed a password reset or believe this notification was sent to you in error, please contact the Mastercard IT Support team through the usual internal support channel.
>
> Thank you for helping us keep Mastercard’s systems and information secure.
>
> Kind regards,  
> Mastercard IT Support Team

## Learning points
The improved simulation demonstrates context and personalization, urgency, impersonation of a trusted team, account-security themes, masked hyperlinks, and professional language and formatting.

**Simulation note:** This was a controlled learning exercise. A real simulation link should resolve to an authorized training destination rather than collect real credentials.
